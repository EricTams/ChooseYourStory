# Razzy and the Buried Secret — Act One roadmap

Planning baseline with implementation notes. Act One now has 32 illustrated, connected screens through the fall. The playable JSON and act-one-script.md contain the current prose, choices and scene descriptions; earlier proposals below are superseded where they differ.

## Dramatic purpose

Razzy is an undersized orange tabby at Thistlewick Academy for Aspiring Adventurers. He feels overlooked and wants to do something that will make people recognize him as an adventurer. His curiosity and powers of observation are already useful; he has not learned to trust them without applause.

Act One follows the last school day before the Grand Archaeological Dig. It establishes a school worth protecting, the physical features Razzy will later exploit, two suspicious visitors, and his affection for Professor Burrowes. It ends with Razzy falling beneath the school. Discovery of the founder's chamber, the departure, and the siege belong to later acts.

The intended emotional progression across the whole story is: “Nobody notices me” → “I'll make them notice me” → “I can do something that matters, even if nobody sees.” Act One mainly inhabits the first two stages.

Home Alone influence: a familiar building, comic adult incompetence, physical setups with delayed payoffs, and two burglars who assume a small child cannot trouble them. Keep the academy's archaeology, secret passages, and betrayal its own story. Classroom errors should include entertaining experimentation; boldness itself is not the mistake. The winning approaches involve noticing something concrete.

The screen outline below records the planning beats. For current prose and button wording, use [the Act One script](act-one-script.md), synchronized with the story JSON. The clarity cleanup removes narrator quips and keeps the choice clues explicit.

## Continuity decisions proposed for this draft

- Everyone is due to leave the academy for three days, including domestic staff supporting the field camp. There is no knowingly unsupervised child or unexplained resident caretaker left behind.
- Razzy does not make the coveted excavation team. Junior students still travel and help at base camp; Burrowes personally assigns Razzy to travel with his equipment group. This preserves both disappointment and an expected place on the trip.
- Burrowes is responsible for checking his group's passengers. Later he falsely certifies Razzy as aboard. Do not show that deliberate act in Act One; reveal it with the betrayal. We can establish the departing carriages in Act Two without showing the marked name.
- Burrowes invites Razzy's interest in the missing room but does not engineer the fall. He exploits Razzy's disappearance the following morning. His plans before that point assume the entire school will travel normally.
- Burrowes alone treats Razzy's report and curiosity with sustained attention. Other adults are busy, sometimes mistaken, and capable of warmth. Avoid a school where everyone is a bully.
- The first early sighting is through a kitchen window toward the treeline. The same pair then appear as peddlers at the gate, making identification possible rather than relying on a vague hunch.
- A daytime archival drawing in Burrowes's office shows a missing room but no usable secret routes. It is not the founder's complete plans, which Razzy finds underground in Act Two.
- The last flagstone is a disused access hatch with a corroded hinge. It tilts when Razzy lifts its ring and the lip beneath him breaks. Ordinary classrooms remain structurally sound.
- Razzy is told to take his messy paste pot to the kitchen and clean it after kitchen duty. He leaves it beside the basin and gets distracted. The sticky paste remains there for later use; it is not a prize or a fresh jar in his pouch.
- Main references: hood-up Razzy; pink-eyed, glasses-wearing weasel; scrappy ferret. Burrowes has broad round tortoiseshell spectacles, visually distinct from the weasel's thin rectangular brass pair. The spectacles clue must stay unambiguous.

## Choice and retry rules

- Each screen has one dominant imageable beat. Setup, consequence, and emotional response can receive separate screens.
- Single buttons advance a scene; they do not pretend to be branching choices.
- Every multi-choice screen offers forward progress. A bad choice may earn a comic consequence, but there are no permanent failure endings in this act.
- Each retry returns directly to its originating decision. Failure consequences reset with that return: no persistent missing equipment, injuries, mess, or penalties.
- Use “Let's try that again.” as provisional retry wording throughout this outline. Final labels may be more playful, but must not suggest walking elsewhere when the scene actually rewinds.
- The gate encounter has two successful routes. They merge only once both put Razzy on his way to tell Burrowes. Later text must not assume he acquired the route-specific observations from both.
- No inventory flags, relationship meters, hidden checks, or conditional buttons are needed.
